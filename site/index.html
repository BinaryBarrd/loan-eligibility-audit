          Compare & findings

          Two popular loan-eligibility calculators give the same person very different answers. Here is what each one assumes.

          Four example people

          Same person, different “You are eligible” (5-year loan). Every amount below was seen on the sites, except one cell marked rule.

          Person Bajaj Paisabazaar at 10% Paisabazaar at 20%
          A: 29, ₹40k income, ₹10k EMIs ₹4,00,000 ₹5,64,784 ₹4,52,935
          B: 29, ₹40k income, no EMIs ₹4,00,000 ₹9,60,000 ₹8,30,380
          C: 35, ₹60k income, ₹35k EMIs ₹4,70,654 not eligible (debts too high) not eligible (rule: the debt check doesn’t use the rate)

          What we found

          • Not AI, just fixed formulas. Each calculator follows one fixed formula, and it runs in your browser: changing the inputs sends nothing to the sites’ servers.
          • No credit score. Neither calculator asks for a credit score or any credit history, though both word the result as your eligibility.
          • The assumptions are not shown next to the result. Bajaj assumes a yearly interest rate of 10% over 5 years, and that up to 75% of income can go to monthly instalments plus expenses. Paisabazaar assumes up to 55% of income can go to monthly instalments, and caps the loan at 24 × monthly income. Neither result says so.
          • City makes no difference. Bajaj requires a city, but any city, even a made-up one (“Xyzabc”), gives the same amount.
          • Tenure is silently capped. Paisabazaar’s tenure slider goes to 10 years, but it never uses more than 5. Choose 6 or 7 and it shows “Eligible Tenure 5 Years”.
          • “Eligible” for ₹47. Both calculators say “You are eligible” for as little as ₹47, when only ₹1 a month of room is left. Bajaj also says “You are eligible for a personal loan upto ₹0”.
          • The page names inputs it doesn’t ask for. Paisabazaar’s page says it estimates “based on your income, employment type, monthly expenses and more”. The calculator asks for neither employment type nor monthly expenses.

          How we tested [DRAFT]

          On 14 November 2026 we opened each calculator in a normal web browser. We typed in different values (income, expenses or EMIs, age, interest rate, tenure), usually changing one thing at a time, and wrote down what the calculator showed. From these input–output pairs we worked out the rule each calculator follows. We then tested each rule on inputs we had not used to build it. Across 76 checks, the rules matched what the sites showed to within ₹1 of rounding.

          We did not log in, enter a PAN, phone number or OTP, or submit any application. We only used the calculators on the public pages. Changing the inputs sends nothing to the sites’ servers: the calculation happens in the browser. So these are fixed formulas, not AI, and not a real loan decision.

          Limits [DRAFT]

          • Both sites call their results “indicative”. These are calculators, not loan offers. Real lenders also look at credit score, job, documents and more.
          • We tested two calculators on one day. Others may work differently, and these two may change at any time.
          • The rules match every case we tried, but we did not see the sites’ code. A rule could differ for inputs we didn’t try.
          • Bajaj asks for “monthly expenses” and Paisabazaar for “existing EMIs”. In the comparison we use the same number for both.
