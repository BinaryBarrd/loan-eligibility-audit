Same person, different “You are eligible”

We tested nine personal-loan eligibility calculators and worked out the rule each one follows. Each page below recreates one calculator, shows the rule behind its number, and lists what the audit found.

Key takeaways

  1. Same person, very different answers. For someone earning ₹40,000 a month with ₹10,000 of EMIs, the calculators showed from ₹1,88,305 (IDFC FIRST Bank) to ₹6,00,000 (Kotak Mahindra Bank). Across 12 standard people, the largest answer was 2.2× to 6.3× the smallest. For 3 of them one calculator said ₹0 while another said several lakh.
  2. Fixed formulas, not AI, and not a credit check. Every calculator follows one fixed formula, run in your browser. Changing the inputs sends nothing to the company. None of the nine asks for a credit score, though most pages say credit score matters.
  3. The assumptions are hidden. Each one assumes a different interest rate (none for Kotak and Axis; 9.99% IDFC FIRST; 10% Bajaj; 14% Poonawalla; 24% Fibe; your own choice on SMFG, Paisabazaar and Aditya Birla) and a different share of income for EMIs (from 55% to 75%). No result says which.
  4. Some boxes change nothing. Bajaj’s required city, Kotak’s tenure slider, Poonawalla’s qualification and Aditya Birla’s “EMI you can afford” do not change the eligible amount.
  5. Some results are odd. Bajaj says “You are eligible” for ₹0. Fibe and Poonawalla make the result disappear. Aditya Birla can leave an old amount on screen. Poonawalla’s “monthly payable amount” is the loan amount. Kotak can show a monthly EMI larger than your income.
  6. Every rule was checked. 1,547 checks across the nine calculators; every comparable one matched what the page showed to within ₹1, including 172 held-out checks not used to build the rules.

The nine calculators

  • Bajaj FinservA lender (non-bank)

    Always uses 10% a year over 5 years, whatever rate or tenure you would pick. The same page advertises rates from 10% to 30.5%.

  • PaisabazaarA loan marketplace

    Assumes up to 55% of your income can go to monthly instalments, counting the ones you already pay.

  • Kotak Mahindra BankA bank

    A plain multiple: 20 times what is left of your income after existing EMIs.

  • IDFC FIRST BankA bank

    Assumes 70% of the income left after all your commitments can go to a new EMI.

  • SMFG India CreditA lender (non-bank)

    Assumes 70% of what is left after EMIs (65% if self-employed) can go to the new EMI.

  • Axis BankA bank

    A step rule: 15 × income, 10 × income or nothing, depending on what share of income your expenses take.

  • FibeA digital lending platform

    Assumes 24% a year.

  • Poonawalla FincorpA lender (non-bank)

    Assumes 14% a year. The page header says “Interest Rate Starting at 9.99%* p.a.”

  • Aditya Birla CapitalA lender (non-bank)

    Two limits, and the smaller wins: a multiple of what is left after expenses (4, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23 for 1 to 7 years), and what an EMI of 65% / 70% / 75% of income would repay.
